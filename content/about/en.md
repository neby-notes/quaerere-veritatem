**Quaerere Veritatem** means, in Latin, "to seek the truth": an activity and its object, not a doctrine. This site is a *digital garden*: a set of independent essays, each studying a concept, an author, a tradition or a question, linked to one another by affinity rather than by discipline. It is the public record of a first-person search, written slowly and with no pretension other than sharing what has been contemplated. I write it in Spanish and in English, in equal parts.

## The search

Seeking the truth is understood here as an **orientation**, not as indefinite curiosity or the accumulation of data: a desire that tends toward a term — contemplation — and that transforms whoever pursues it.

That desire has two poles that sustain one another, both formulated already at the origins of the tradition: the longing to know for the sheer pleasure of understanding — "all men by nature desire to know," says Aristotle at the opening of *Metaphysics* (I, 1, 980a21) — and the examination of one's own life, which Socrates turns into a programme in Plato's *Apology*: "the unexamined life is not livable for man" (38a). For an unexamined life hardly sustains an honest search.

It is not merely a matter of the intellect. Saint Augustine puts it at the opening of *Confessions*: "You made us for yourself, and our heart is restless until it rests in you" (I, 1, 1). That restlessness does not fully come to rest until it finds what it was made for. That is why truth is treated here in several registers at once: the propositional, which asks what makes a statement true; the personal, which lives it as an orientation of desire; and the artistic, which seeks it in works that imitate and transfigure reality.

## Why there are no disciplines

The premise is the same one that opens this site: reality is one, and disciplines are not borders within it but perspectives of a finite intellect before an infinitely complex object. Organizing knowledge by disciplines is an instrumental decision, not a necessity of the object. That is why there are no sections of philosophy, theology or aesthetics here, but trails that cut across them: true knowledge is not found in the sharpness observed from a single perspective, but in the capacity to comprehend the object from all its angles.

What does exist here are tags, and they are not disciplines through the back door. A philosophy section delimits a territory and excludes everything else; a tag, by contrast, excludes nothing: it arises when the content needs it, coexists with others within a single essay and disappears when it is no longer needed. Its function is not to classify but to interconnect: to gather pieces that, wherever they come from, treat kindred matters. Together with the references between essays — each piece points to those that illuminate what it touches — tags form the garden's living cartography: dynamic, open to revision, and faithful to the idea that reality does not come pre-divided.

## Contemplating and transmitting

Intellectual work is understood here through the old formula of the Dominican tradition:

> *Contemplata aliis tradere* — "to hand on to others what has been contemplated". Saint Thomas Aquinas, *Summa theologiae*, II-II, q. 188, a. 6.

One investigates in order to understand, and one publishes in order to hand on to others what has been understood. What comes to light is not hasty opinion, but what has been thought, doubted and reread. And transmission is also a communal task: the truth is not sought for oneself, but to be given.

In all of this I do not claim to be a bearer of the truth, though I do try to seek it: I start from the humility of my ignorance and from the tireless desire to learn that I find in my faith and in my reason. What you will find here are not closed systems, but slight directions toward what I believe is right. Nor are they definitive: no essay will ever be finished. I will keep working on them — changing them, refining them, remaking them —, because knowledge and comprehension admit no final point; there is always time to learn something that improves what one thought one understood.

## To the reader

Whatever road has brought you here, may you find in these pages a question to keep you company, a reading that does you good or, at the very least, a moment of calm. 

May your own search take you far and bring you back with your hands full.
